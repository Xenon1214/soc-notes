Types of Threats

In cybersecurity, a threat is anything that can potentially cause damage to a system or data. It usually works together with a vulnerability.

One of the most common threats is malware. This is any malicious software like trojans, worms or ransomware. For example, a user downloads a file that looks normal but actually installs ransomware and locks all files.

Phishing is another very common threat. It’s when attackers trick users into giving away sensitive information. Usually it comes as an email that looks legit, like from a bank or service, but leads to a fake website.

Brute force attacks are simple but effective. The attacker just tries many password combinations until one works. In logs this looks like many failed login attempts in a short time.

DDoS attacks are about overwhelming a system with traffic so it stops working. The goal is not to break in, but to make the service unavailable.

Social engineering is more about people than technology. The attacker manipulates someone into giving access or information. For example, pretending to be IT support.

There are also insider threats, when someone inside the company causes harm, either intentionally or by mistake.

In SOC, you deal with all of these by analyzing logs, alerts and behavior patterns. The main goal is to understand what kind of threat you are looking at and how serious it is.
